Earning Capacity Assessment

Comprehensive analysis of pre and post-injury earning potential for disability and personal injury cases across Massachusetts and Upstate New York

What is an Earning Capacity Assessment?

An earning capacity assessment is a specialized vocational economic evaluation that determines an individual's ability to earn wages before and after an injury, illness, or disability. Our certified experts provide objective, data-driven analyses that quantify the economic impact of physical or cognitive limitations on lifetime earning potential.

These assessments are critical components in personal injury litigation, disability insurance claims, workers' compensation cases, and divorce proceedings. Our evaluations combine vocational expertise with economic methodology to produce defensible opinions that stand up to scrutiny in legal proceedings throughout Massachusetts and Upstate New York.

Our Earning Capacity Analysis Process

1. Pre-Injury Earning Capacity Analysis

We establish baseline earning potential through comprehensive review of education, work history, skills, and career trajectory prior to the disabling event.

2. Medical Documentation Review

Our experts analyze medical records, functional capacity evaluations, and physician opinions to understand the nature and extent of limitations.

3. Post-Injury Vocational Assessment

We evaluate remaining work capabilities, transferable skills, and accommodation needs to determine residual earning capacity.

4. Labor Market Analysis

Using current data from Massachusetts and New York labor markets, we identify realistic employment options and wage potential.

5. Economic Loss Calculation

We calculate the difference between pre and post-injury earning capacity, including considerations for benefits, work-life expectancy, and economic factors.

6. Comprehensive Reporting

Our detailed reports present clear methodologies, supporting data, and defensible conclusions suitable for litigation or settlement negotiations.

Key Components of Our Assessments

Vocational Factors
