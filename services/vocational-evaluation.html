Vocational Evaluation Services

Comprehensive assessments to determine vocational potential, transferable skills, and rehabilitation needs following injury or disability

What is a Vocational Evaluation?

A vocational evaluation is a comprehensive, systematic process designed to assess an individual's work-related abilities, limitations, and potential for employment. Our certified vocational experts conduct thorough evaluations that provide objective, evidence-based opinions about vocational capacity and rehabilitation potential.

These evaluations are crucial in various legal contexts, including disability insurance claims, workers' compensation cases, personal injury litigation, and Social Security disability determinations. Our assessments help legal professionals, insurance companies, and individuals understand the impact of injury or disability on work capacity and earning potential.

Our Vocational Evaluation Process

1. Initial Review & Planning

We begin with a comprehensive review of medical records, employment history, educational background, and relevant case documentation to develop a customized evaluation plan.

2. Clinical Interview

Our experts conduct in-depth interviews to gather information about work history, education, skills, interests, and the impact of the disability on daily activities and work functions.

3. Vocational Testing

We administer standardized vocational tests to assess aptitudes, interests, academic achievement, and work-related abilities. Testing is tailored to each individual's specific circumstances.

4. Transferable Skills Analysis

Using industry-standard methodologies, we identify skills gained from past work experience that can transfer to other occupations within the individual's current capabilities.

5. Labor Market Research

We analyze local and national labor markets to identify realistic employment opportunities that match the individual's skills, abilities, and limitations.

6. Report Development

Our comprehensive reports detail findings, vocational opinions, and recommendations, providing clear, defensible conclusions for use in legal proceedings.

Types of Vocational Evaluations

Initial Vocational Assessment

Comprehensive evaluation to establish baseline vocational capacity and identify rehabilitation needs following injury or disability onset.

Employability Evaluation

Assessment focused on determining whether an individual can perform any work in the competitive labor market given their limitations.

Return-to-Work Assessment

Evaluation of an individual's ability to return to their previous job or alternative employment with or without accommodations.

Transferable Skills Analysis

Detailed analysis of work history to identify skills that transfer to other occupations within physical and mental capabilities.

Job Placement Feasibility

Assessment of the likelihood of successful job placement considering local labor market conditions and individual factors.

Rehabilitation Plan Development

Creation of detailed vocational rehabilitation plans including training recommendations and return-to-work strategies.

Key Components of Our Evaluations

  • Medical Records Review: Comprehensive analysis of medical documentation to understand functional limitations
  • Educational Assessment: Evaluation of academic achievement and potential for further education or training
  • Work History Analysis: Detailed review of past employment, job duties, and acquired skills
  • Psychometric Testing: Standardized assessments of aptitudes, interests, and abilities
  • Functional Capacity Correlation: Integration of physical and cognitive limitations with vocational potential
  • Labor Market Analysis: Research on job availability and wage data in relevant geographic areas
  • Accommodation Assessment: Identification of workplace modifications that could enable employment

When is a Vocational Evaluation Needed?

Vocational evaluations are essential in numerous situations:

  • Determining disability status for insurance claims
  • Assessing work capacity in workers' compensation cases
  • Evaluating earning capacity loss in personal injury litigation
  • Supporting Social Security disability applications or appeals
  • Planning vocational rehabilitation services
  • Determining reasonable accommodations under the ADA
  • Assessing employability in wrongful termination cases
