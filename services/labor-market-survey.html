Labor Market Survey

Data-driven employment opportunity analysis providing real-world job availability and wage information for Massachusetts and Upstate New York markets

What is a Labor Market Survey?

A labor market survey is a systematic analysis of actual job opportunities available to an individual based on their skills, abilities, and limitations. Our surveys go beyond theoretical assessments by identifying real employers, actual job openings, and current wage data in specific geographic areas throughout Massachusetts and Upstate New York.

These surveys are essential in disability cases, workers' compensation claims, and vocational assessments where determining actual employment opportunities is crucial. Our methodology combines database research, direct employer contact, and real-time job market analysis to provide courts and insurance companies with concrete evidence of employment potential.

Our Labor Market Survey Methodology

1. Vocational Profile Development

We analyze the individual's education, work history, transferable skills, and functional limitations to create a comprehensive vocational profile.

2. Occupation Identification

Using O*NET and DOT classifications, we identify suitable occupations that match the individual's capabilities and restrictions.

3. Geographic Market Definition

We define the relevant labor market area based on commuting patterns and local employment concentrations in Massachusetts or New York.

4. Employer Research

Our team identifies actual employers in the defined geographic area who hire for the identified occupations.

5. Job Availability Analysis

We document current job openings, application requirements, physical demands, and wage ranges through multiple data sources.

6. Direct Employer Contact

When appropriate, we contact employers directly to verify job requirements, availability, and willingness to hire individuals with specific limitations.

Types of Labor Market Surveys

Targeted Job Search

Focused analysis of specific occupations identified through transferable skills analysis, documenting actual job openings and requirements.

Wage Loss Analysis

Comparison of pre-injury earning capacity with post-injury employment options, including detailed wage data for alternative occupations.

One-Day Job Search

Simulation of actual job search activities an individual could perform in one day, documenting available positions and application processes.

Industry-Specific Survey

Analysis of employment opportunities within specific industries or sectors relevant to the individual's background and skills.

Entry-Level Survey

Assessment of unskilled or semi-skilled positions available without specific training or experience requirements.

Remote Work Survey

Analysis of work-from-home and remote employment opportunities, increasingly relevant in post-pandemic labor markets.

Key Components of Our Surveys

  • Real Job Listings: Documentation of actual, current job openings with employer names and application information
  • Wage Data: Specific salary ranges from job postings and regional wage surveys
  • Physical Demands: Analysis of job requirements compared to individual's functional limitations
  • Educational Requirements: Documentation of minimum qualifications and preferred credentials
  • Geographic Analysis: Mapping of job locations and commuting requirements
  • Labor Market Trends: Industry growth projections and employment outlook data
  • Employer Information: Company profiles, size, and hiring practices

Data Sources & Methodology

Our labor market surveys utilize multiple authoritative sources:

  • Current job board listings (Indeed, Monster, CareerBuilder, LinkedIn)
  • State job banks (JobQuest for MA, NYS Job Bank)
  • Direct employer websites and recruiting portals
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational data
  • O*NET occupational profiles and requirements
  • State labor market information systems
  • Industry association job boards
  • Local newspaper classifieds and regional job sites

Applications in Legal Cases

Labor market surveys provide crucial evidence in various legal contexts:

  • Demonstrating job availability for disability insurance claims
  • Supporting or refuting claims of total disability
  • Calculating wage loss in personal injury cases
  • Assessing vocational rehabilitation potential
  • Determining reasonable accommodation feasibility
  • Evaluating employability in workers' compensation cases
  • Supporting Social Security disability determinations
